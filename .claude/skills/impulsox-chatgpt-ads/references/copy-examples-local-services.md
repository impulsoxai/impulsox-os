# Copy Examples — Local Services (Calibration)

> Load this with `copywriting-engine.md` before Step 6. Each pair contrasts feed copy (BAD) against conversation copy (GOOD). Study the contrast, then write GOOD-style only.
> Every GOOD obeys the engine's limits (title ≤50 / copy ≤100), specificity rule, and prohibition list. Every claim in a GOOD is one a landing page could prove.

---

### Pair 1 — use_case / exploring

**BAD (feed copy):** `🔥 BEST KOREAN BBQ IN ORLANDO 🔥` / `You HAVE to try this AMAZING spot — don't miss out!!!`
Why it fails: loud, all-caps, emoji-stuffed hype with an unprovable superlative — pure interruption, not the answer the user was already looking for.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `Korean BBQ near Lake Eola` (25) / `Tabletop grills, marinated bulgogi. Walk-ins seated nightly.` (60)
Why it works: AIDA for an unaware browser — Attention is the vivid noun phrase "Korean BBQ near Lake Eola" (proper noun Lake Eola), Interest is the sensory detail "tabletop grills, marinated bulgogi", Desire is the low-friction "walk-ins seated nightly" promise, and the click is the Action.

### Pair 2 — comparison / comparing

**BAD (feed copy):** `THE ONLY GLUTEN-FREE MENU THAT MATTERS` / `Hands down the best GF food anywhere — trust us, it's incredible!`
Why it fails: vague brag built on superlatives ("best", "incredible") with nothing countable — it shouts instead of giving the comparer the concrete edge they came to weigh.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `16 gluten-free dishes, marked` (29) / `Separate fryer, no cross-contact. Order GF with confidence.` (59)
Why it works: FAB for a solution-aware comparer — the Feature (16 GF dishes, separate fryer) yields the Advantage (no cross-contamination) which delivers the Benefit (eat without worry); the copy runs Feature → Advantage → Benefit in order, even-handed and not urgent.

### Pair 3 — cost / planning

**BAD (feed copy):** `UNBEATABLE CATERING DEALS!!!` / `Lowest prices in town — grab this amazing offer before it's gone!`
Why it fails: loud price brag with vague hype ("unbeatable", "amazing") and manufactured urgency — feed noise that gives a planner no usable number to act on.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `Catering trays from $42` (23) / `Order Cuban platters 24h ahead for 10-20 people near Kissimmee.` (63)
Why it works: 4U for a problem-aware planner — Useful is the catering offer sized to the event (10-20 people), Urgent is the "24h ahead" lead-time beat, Unique is the Cuban platters, and Ultra-specific is the $42 price; helpful, not shouty.

> [ILLUSTRATIVE — confirm the GOOD example's real claims with the client before shipping]

### Pair 4 — speed / booking

**BAD (feed copy):** `RESERVE NOW OR REGRET IT FOREVER!!!` / `Tables vanish FAST — book this incredible sushi spot right now!!!`
Why it fails: panic-driven, all-caps urgency with fake scarcity and an empty superlative — billboard pressure that intrudes on a user already ready to act.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `Sushi counter open till 11pm` (28) / `Reserve 8 omakase seats 3 blocks from Universal. Book online.` (61)
Why it works: 4U for a most-aware booker — Useful is the proximity (3 blocks from Universal), Urgent is the limited seats and late hours (8 omakase seats, open till 11pm), Unique is the omakase counter, and Ultra-specific is the exact seat count and hours; frictionless action with one clear "Book online".

### Pair 5 — pain / fixing

**BAD (feed copy):** `AC BROKEN?! WE'RE THE BEST IN ORLANDO!` / `Fastest service ever — call now for amazing same-day repair!!!`
Why it fails: shouty superlatives ("best", "fastest", "amazing") with no real figure — it hypes itself instead of calmly removing the pain the user is feeling right now.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `AC out in the Florida heat?` (27) / `Orlando techs reach you in 90 min, 7 days. Flat $89 diagnostic.` (63)
Why it works: PAS for a problem-aware fixer — the title names the Problem as a question that carries the Agitate beat implicitly, so the copy skips an explicit agitation line and jumps straight to the Solution: a real response time (90 min) and flat price ($89); reassuring and fast.

### Pair 6 — outcome / buying

**BAD (feed copy):** `CRACKED SCREEN? WE FIX IT ALL!!!` / `The most trusted repair shop around — unbeatable speed, guaranteed!`
Why it fails: two distinct problems. First, the all-caps boast and vague hype are a feed-copy tone issue — the validator only WARNs on caps/`!`/emoji and leaves it to a human. Second, the word `guaranteed` is an absolute OpenAI policy violation: a hard FAIL in the validator regardless of tone or channel, so even a calm, conversational "guaranteed" is equally banned — it is not a feed-style problem.

**GOOD (conversation copy):** `Cracked screen, no backup phone?` (32) / `Most iPhone screens fixed in 30 min while you wait in Winter Park.` (66)
Why it works: BAB for a solution-aware buyer — the Before is the stranded user, the After/Bridge is a concrete turnaround (30 min, in Winter Park) that justifies the pick; confident and de-risking without absolute promises.
